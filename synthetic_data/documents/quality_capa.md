# Quality Nonconformance and CAPA Procedure

A nonconformance record must identify the affected product or process, detection point, quantity, containment, disposition, and responsible owner. Immediate containment is required before normal production resumes when safety, regulatory compliance, or customer fit and function may be affected.

A formal corrective and preventive action is required for a repeat major nonconformance, any critical nonconformance, an adverse regulatory finding, a customer escape with material impact, or a systemic trend. Root-cause analysis must distinguish the occurrence cause, escape cause, and systemic cause. Acceptable methods include five whys, fishbone analysis, fault-tree analysis, and designed experiments.

The CAPA owner defines actions, due dates, evidence, and effectiveness criteria. Effectiveness is normally verified between 30 and 90 days after implementation using objective data. The person verifying effectiveness must be independent of the action owner when practical. CAPA closure requires Quality approval.
