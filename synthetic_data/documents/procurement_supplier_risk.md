# Procurement and Supplier Risk Procedure

## Supplier tiers
Tier A suppliers are critical to production continuity, product safety, cybersecurity, or regulatory compliance. Tier B suppliers provide important but substitutable goods or services. Tier C suppliers have low operational criticality.

## Due diligence
Before onboarding a Tier A supplier, Procurement must complete financial viability, sanctions, beneficial ownership, information security, business continuity, quality, and capacity assessments. Tier A suppliers handling Confidential or Restricted data require a security assessment and approved contractual controls before data exchange. Tier A risk reviews are refreshed annually; Tier B reviews every two years; Tier C reviews when a material change occurs.

## Performance and escalation
Supplier scorecards include on-time delivery, parts per million defects, responsiveness, capacity adherence, corrective-action closure, and financial risk. A Tier A supplier that misses the same critical KPI for two consecutive months must submit a recovery plan within ten business days. A production-stopping risk requires immediate executive escalation and a dual-source or inventory-containment decision.

## Purchase authorization
Purchase orders above $250,000 require approval from the business vice president and Finance. Purchases above $1 million also require Chief Procurement Officer approval. Splitting purchase orders to avoid approval thresholds is prohibited.
